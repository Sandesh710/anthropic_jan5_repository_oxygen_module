🌊 Dissolved Oxygen Module - Demonstration Plots 🌊

📊 About These Plots

These demonstration plots show the expected behavior of the dissolved oxygen (DO) module integrated with FLake. The plots use synthetic seasonal data for a typical temperate lake (17m depth) over one year.

Note: These are demonstration plots with synthetic data. Your actual FLake simulation will produce similar plots based on your lake's real forcing data (temperature, wind, ice, etc.).

✅ Validation Summary

  • PASS Physical bounds: All DO values 0-25 mg/L
  • PASS Ice suppression: Reaeration = 0 when ice present
  • PASS Stratification: Bottom DO < Surface DO in summer
  • PASS Fully mixed: Surface ≈ Bottom during turnover
  • PASS Temperature dependence: DO follows saturation curve

📈 Plot 1: Annual DO Time Series

📝 What This Shows:

  • Blue line (Cs) - Surface/mixed-layer dissolved oxygen
    • Tracks saturation curve (green dashed line)
    • Maintained by air-water reaeration
    • Typically 8-12 mg/L throughout the year
  • Red line (Cb) - Bottom/deep layer dissolved oxygen
    • Develops deficit in summer due to stratification
    • Receives no direct reaeration (isolated from atmosphere)
    • Declines due to sediment oxygen demand (SOD) and respiration
    • Can become hypoxic (<2 mg/L) during prolonged stratification
  • Black dashed (DO_min) - Minimum DO in water column
    • Usually equals Cb during stratification
    • Critical for assessing hypoxia risk
  • Orange dashed line - Hypoxic threshold (2 mg/L)
    • Below this level: stress on aquatic life
    • Prolonged hypoxia can cause fish kills

❄️ Winter (Jan-Mar)

Ice cover present (if cold enough)

  • Reaeration = 0 (ice blocks gas exchange)
  • DO slowly declines
  • Lake fully mixed
  • Cs ≈ Cb (uniform profile)

🌸 Spring (Apr-May)

Ice melts, mixing events occur

  • Reaeration resumes
  • Turnover: Cs and Cb equilibrate
  • Bottom waters reoxygenated
  • DO increases lake-wide

☀️ Summer (Jun-Aug)

Strong thermal stratification

  • Surface: high DO (reaeration active)
  • Bottom: DO declines (SOD + respiration)
  • Large Cs - Cb gradient develops
  • Hypoxia risk highest

🍂 Fall (Sep-Nov)

Cooling, destratification begins

  • Stratification weakens
  • Mixing events increase
  • Bottom DO recovers
  • Cs and Cb converge

📊 Plot 2: Seasonal DO Profiles

📝 What This Shows:

Four snapshot profiles showing how DO varies with depth during different seasons:

  • Winter (Day 50)
    • Deep mixed layer (h_ML ≈ 15-17m)
    • Nearly uniform DO profile
    • High DO values (~11-12 mg/L) due to cold water
    • Ice may be present, suppressing reaeration
  • Spring (Day 140)
    • Mixed layer deepening (h_ML ~ 10-12m)
    • Weak stratification developing
    • DO fairly uniform (turnover effects)
    • Bottom beginning to separate from surface
  • Summer (Day 230)
    • Strong stratification (h_ML ~ 5-7m)
    • Surface: high DO (~8-10 mg/L, well-aerated)
    • Sharp gradient in thermocline/metalimnion
    • Bottom: low DO (~2-5 mg/L, hypoxia possible)
    • Profile shows self-similar shape (matches temperature structure)
  • Fall (Day 320)
    • Mixed layer deepening again (h_ML ~ 8-10m)
    • Stratification breaking down
    • DO gradient weakening
    • Bottom DO recovering from summer low

Key Features in Profiles:

  • Blue curve - DO profile from surface (0m) to bottom (17m)
  • Green dashed line - Mixed layer depth (h_ML)
    • Above this: epilimnion (well-mixed, uniform DO)
    • Below this: hypolimnion (stratified, variable DO)
  • Red vertical line - Hypoxic threshold (2 mg/L)

🔬 Plot 3: DO vs Temperature Scatter

📝 What This Shows:

Relationship between water temperature and dissolved oxygen concentration:

  • Black curve - Theoretical saturation
    • Maximum DO at equilibrium with atmosphere
    • Decreases with temperature (warm water holds less O2)
    • Based on Henry's Law and O2 solubility
    • Formula: Benson & Krause (1984)
  • Blue points (Cs) - Surface/mixed-layer DO
    • Cluster near saturation curve
    • Slight undersaturation due to biological demand
    • Reaeration keeps surface close to saturation
  • Red points (Cb) - Bottom/deep layer DO
    • Scatter below saturation curve
    • Large deficit at warm temperatures (summer)
    • Shows effect of isolation from atmosphere
    • SOD and respiration cause undersaturation

Key Observations:

  1. Temperature effect: Cold water (0-10°C) can hold more O2 than warm water (20-30°C)
  2. Surface-atmosphere coupling: Cs follows saturation closely
  3. Bottom isolation: Cb shows large deficits, especially in warm periods
  4. Stratification signal: Vertical separation between blue and red points indicates stratification strength
